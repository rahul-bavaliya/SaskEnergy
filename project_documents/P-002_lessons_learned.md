# P-002 Lessons Learned

The team managed mall coordination professionally and avoided major disruption to adjacent tenants. Temporary protection, daily cleanup, and communication with mall operations were strong. The finished store opened successfully, and the owner was satisfied with the customer-facing space.

The main estimate variance came from access and after-hours requirements. The original estimate treated mall constraints as a manageable coordination issue, but the actual rules were stricter: freight elevator use required reservations, the loading dock had limited windows, noisy demolition was restricted to overnight periods, and several shutdowns had to occur after public hours. These constraints increased labor premiums, supervision, temporary protection, and repeated fixture moves.

Future mall retail projects should include a formal access review before pricing. Estimators should verify loading paths, freight elevator capacity, allowable work hours, noise restrictions, staging rules, and whether phased occupancy is required. A simple similarity search may over-rank this project for future retail build-outs because the scope language is similar, but the access constraints make it a weaker comparator for an open suburban plaza space.

The team also learned that mall security escort requirements can create small daily delays that compound across a short schedule.
