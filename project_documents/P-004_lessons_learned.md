# P-004 Lessons Learned

The team performed well on owner coordination, room-by-room scope tracking, and casework integration. Early meetings with clinical staff helped catch several workflow issues before framing was complete. The final space met the owner's operational needs and passed required inspections.

Cost and schedule pressure came from specialty systems and inspection interpretation. Treatment rooms required more exhaust coordination and controls work than anticipated. Accessibility review also produced changes to washroom clearances, door hardware, and circulation details after the pricing basis was set. These changes were not catastrophic, but they created rework across several trades.

Future projects with specialty rooms should include more explicit assumptions around inspection standards, controls sequences, plumbing density, accessible clearances, and owner equipment interfaces. Estimators should avoid treating all tenant improvements as interchangeable just because the work includes partitions, finishes, HVAC, electrical, and plumbing.

For retail build-out comparisons, this project is useful for understanding interior complexity and MEP coordination, but candidates should discount medical-specific systems. A practical AI tool should explain why the project is partially comparable rather than simply ranking it by square footage or total cost.
