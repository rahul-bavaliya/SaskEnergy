# P-001 Lessons Learned

The project team delivered a strong retail build-out and the sales floor layout, fixture coordination, and finish sequencing worked well. Early mockups helped the owner make timely finish decisions, and weekly coordination with the fixture vendor reduced conflicts in the back-of-house and point-of-sale areas.

The largest estimate variance came from electrical capacity. The team relied on landlord-provided utility information during estimating, but a late utility review showed that the existing service could not support the final lighting, POS, security, and back-of-house equipment loads. The resulting service upgrade, temporary power, retesting, and resequencing created cost and schedule pressure.

Future similar projects should validate utility capacity before pricing is finalized, not only during construction. Estimators should request panel schedules, service size, transformer information, and recent load data during due diligence. They should also carry a clearer allowance for low-voltage pathways, security rough-ins, and owner equipment coordination.

One cost driver that was not obvious in the estimate was the owner's security vendor requirement for dedicated conduit paths to all entry points and cash-handling areas. That coordination created small but repeated ceiling rework and should be surfaced for future retail build-outs.
