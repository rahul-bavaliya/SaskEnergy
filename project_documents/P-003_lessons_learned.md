# P-003 Lessons Learned

The project benefited from clear site access and an unoccupied work area. Structural sequencing, roofing, and base-building utility coordination were handled well. The shell was delivered with enough flexibility for future tenant improvements, which reduced downstream conflicts for leasing.

Estimate variance was driven mainly by civil and structural issues. Stormwater revisions, soil export, and added canopy steel increased cost after the original budget was set. Utility connection fees also came in above allowance. These are important lessons for ground-up retail construction, but they are less directly useful for tenant build-outs where the base building already exists.

Candidates should be careful when comparing this project to newer opportunities. It is older than the other historical projects, so raw cost numbers may understate current pricing because of escalation. It also has a different cost profile: site preparation and structure dominate the project, while interior finishes and tenant systems are comparatively limited.

Future use of this project should focus on broad retail delivery lessons, not direct cost benchmarking for tenant improvement work. The best reusable insight is that early utility and authority coordination can materially affect cost, even when the source of that risk differs by project type.
