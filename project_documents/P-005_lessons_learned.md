# P-005 Lessons Learned

The team handled occupied-site coordination well and kept warehouse operations running during construction. Temporary barriers, delivery planning, and weekly shutdown coordination helped reduce disruption. Office finish quality was on track at the time of the latest project review.

The project is not fully closed, so actual cost conclusions are limited. Partial actuals show pressure in HVAC, electrical, plumbing, and slab trenching, but final invoices, owner changes, and reconciliation are still incomplete. This makes the project risky as a benchmark unless the tool clearly labels confidence and data completeness.

The largest known issue came from incomplete record drawings. Underground sanitary routing did not match expectations, which required additional slab trenching and coordination around active warehouse operations. Rooftop unit procurement also slipped, creating temporary heating costs.

Future similar projects should validate underground utilities and long-lead equipment earlier. For retail build-out analysis, this project can provide lessons about existing-building uncertainty, washroom upgrades, HVAC zoning, and electrical distribution, but it should not be treated as a primary cost comparator because it is a warehouse office conversion with incomplete actuals.
